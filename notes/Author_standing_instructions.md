# Author Standing Instructions

These are Sijie Wang's standing instructions for economics paper writing and revision, consolidated on 2026-11-03 from the author's project instructions. Read this file before every substantive manuscript-writing or revision task, together with `EconTheoryWriting_principles.md` and the repository README. Current explicit user instructions govern the current task; these author-specific instructions refine the general writing references. They apply across manuscripts unless a rule explicitly names one manuscript. Manuscript-specific facts are examples, not definitions to impose on other papers.

# Manuscript editing workflow

- Before every manuscript edit, fetch the target file from the current GitHub `main` branch and use those exact bytes as the base.
- Whenever reopening a manuscript, compare the fetched version with the last reviewed or pushed version and identify the author's intervening changes before editing. Never restore an older local draft over those changes.
- Treat the author's GitHub/Overleaf wording as authoritative. Make local, minimal changes on top of it; do not replace whole paragraphs merely to polish prose.
- Before pushing, fetch GitHub again. If the blob SHA changed, reapply the local patch to the new version and resolve the differences before any update.
- Review a word-level diff against the fetched base. Preserve every author addition; scrutinize and justify every deletion or substantive replacement.
- Compile and inspect the affected PDF pages, push only the intended manuscript file, then read it back from GitHub and verify byte-for-byte equality.

# Academic writing principles

- For substantial drafting or restructuring, first identify the question, why it matters, the precise contribution, the mechanism or method, the closest prior work, and the implications. Build the paper around one or two results the reader should remember. Classify other results as supporting propositions, proof lemmas, or extensions according to their role.
- Follow the author's inverted-pyramid structure: the title gives the most condensed central message; the abstract gives the core argument; the introduction expands that same argument; and the body supplies full definitions, assumptions, results, and proofs. In the introduction, develop the abstract's core claims in a logically corresponding sequence, adding the scope, reasons, mechanisms, and significance readers need at that level.
- Review the abstract and introduction together. Check that each core abstract claim receives an informative expansion in the introduction, with emphasis proportional to its role in the paper. An introduction should explain the actual finding and its meaning rather than replace an abstract conclusion with a vague announcement such as "we identify when." This correspondence concerns the argument, not a mechanical requirement that each abstract sentence have its own paragraph. Secondary results should support the main sequence without interrupting it.
- Preserve each core claim's economic and argumentative role across these layers, as well as its mathematical content. For example, if the abstract emphasizes that a correction preserves a familiar economic intuition, the introduction and body should explain that continuity; merely restating the corresponding monotonicity result loses part of the argument.
- Give the introduction a coherent narrative: motivation, contribution, informal model, main findings and their economic meaning, and relation to the closest literature. Make each paragraph perform a clear task. Treat this as a flexible structure, not a mandatory sequence of stock sentences; avoid sweeping importance or novelty claims without support.
- Present a game through its players, actions, information, and preferences, and introduce essential assumptions when the model is specified. Explain the economic role of assumptions proportionately. Prefer a stable core model and introduce variants as clearly identified extensions.
- Motivate parameter restrictions with a few accessible economic applications and explain what the parameter values mean for behavior. Do not reconstruct another paper's model or introduce its primitive notation when that detail is unnecessary for the motivation. Distinguish assumptions actually maintained in cited work from boundary cases or extensions allowed by the present paper.
- Organize explanations in the order a reader needs them: define the objects and comparison rule, define the relevant concept, introduce notation, then state the result. Do not assume familiarity merely because the author and agent have discussed a concept repeatedly.
- Give definitions their full mathematical content. For example, a least element must both belong to the set and be no greater than every element under the stated order. Distinguish a coordinatewise infimum from a least element; finiteness alone does not guarantee the latter.
- Put substantive conclusions requiring proof in the proposition or theorem. Before the result, give definitions, assumptions, and necessary motivation; avoid announcing those conclusions with phrases such as "We prove that" when they belong in the result itself.
- Clarify by supplying missing information and logical connections. Do not expand one sentence into several paraphrases of the same point. Preserve qualifications essential to the meaning even when shortening prose.
- Use established, familiar terminology with explicit referents. Avoid invented labels, unexplained synonyms, and shorthand that requires readers to reconstruct an earlier argument. Introduce notation only when it will be used, and check its meaning throughout the manuscript.
- Before changing a term or its interpretation, trace its definitions and uses through the introduction, model, results, and appendices. Distinguish an explicit definition from an interpretation you inferred. Report inconsistencies to the author; do not silently resolve them by redefining the author's terminology. In `uniqueness.tex`, an autarkic optimum is the optimal action without network interactions before imposing action bounds, so it may be negative in extensions; under the maintained assumption $0\leq q_i<b_i$, it coincides with the feasible isolated optimum.
- Review each edit in the context of the surrounding section and the paper's economic argument. Connect formulas to their economic implications, remove nearby repetition, and keep definitions, conclusions, interpretations, and proofs in their appropriate places.
- Discuss related work through the question, assumptions, conclusions, and exact value added. Describe prior results accurately and generously. For a correction, identify the precise limitation and its consequences without claiming unsupported effects on subsequent literature.
- In proofs, identify what remains to be shown and make the substantive implications explicit. Use logical transitions where they explain a dependency, without narrating routine algebra or repeating established results.
- Preserve mathematical scope when simplifying: existence versus at most one solution, positive versus boundary solutions, strict versus weak inequalities, local versus global results, and finite changes versus derivatives. A shorter statement must not silently strengthen the theorem.
- Keep the scope of claims consistent across the abstract, introduction, economic interpretation, and conclusion while expanding detail progressively. The abstract is not a checklist of every assumption or restriction: include qualifications needed to prevent a materially misleading claim, and develop other maintained assumptions in the introduction and body. Do not treat the omission of a detailed restriction from the abstract as an inconsistency by itself.
- Read downstream results, proofs, and examples under the clearly stated maintained assumptions. Failure to repeat a global parameter restriction is not itself an inconsistency. Repeat a restriction only when it prevents a material ambiguity or distinguishes an extension; do not add clutter by treating each sentence as independent of the model.
- Where useful, translate abstract conditions into expressions or checks involving model primitives. Explain how the relevant parts of a formula express the economic mechanism, so readers can see both what the condition means and how to apply it.
- The governing principle for examples is to use as few as possible and make them as simple as possible. Each example must serve a specific claim. Ideally, one simple running example should support the paper from beginning to end, illustrating several claims as they arise. Before adding or retaining a separate example, first see whether the running example can be reused or modestly adapted. A distinct explanatory role alone does not justify another example; add one only when the needed point cannot be conveyed clearly with the existing setup. Treat a single running example as the preferred ideal, not a rigid requirement that sacrifices clarity. For routine checks such as an example's spectral restriction, report the computed value briefly and keep verification details in working notes unless they are needed to understand the example.
- Keep a consistent visual style and scale across figures in each manuscript: use common fonts and label sizes, node dimensions, edge widths and arrowheads, colors and their meanings, component frames, panel labels, and caption formatting. Match the physical sizes of comparable elements at final print size; adapt layouts to different networks or panel counts while preserving readable labels and undistorted diagrams. Reuse shared TikZ styles where practical, and inspect related compiled figures side by side for consistency, spacing, readability, and overlaps. This principle is also recorded in https://github.com/econstill/theory-writing/blob/main/notes/EconTheoryWriting_principles.md#figures-and-visual-consistency (added 2026-11-01).
- Reserve the main text for the central argument and the explanations needed to understand it. Put technical or repetitive proofs and supporting extensions in the appendix according to the author's manuscript-specific preferences. Every retained appendix item should have a clear role; necessary definitions belong in the text, not in footnotes.
- Treat the author's corrections as guidance for future edits across manuscripts. Apply the underlying principle while respecting differences in models and notation; do not mechanically copy formulations between papers.
